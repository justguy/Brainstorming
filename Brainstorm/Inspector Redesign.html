Five rules for any "inspector-style" drawer

01
Provenance first
Top of the drawer is where did this come from, can I undo it, what did it cost. Users open the inspector to trust, not to configure.
02
Journey, not grid
Lifecycle steps are a walk. A horizontal strip with done/active/locked states tells the story. A 2×6 grid of boxes doesn't.
03
Only the active step speaks
The NOW card is the only step that gets voice, text, and actions. Locked steps stay dots. No narrating emptiness.
04
Don't cover the board
Inspector is a right-side drawer, not a full modal. Ink tether from the idea shows what the drawer is about without hiding context.
05
Let Dev tell the story
Every row in the timeline is dated, typed, signed by a role (Scout/Synthesizer/etc.), and reversible. The inspector is the turn log, not a config panel.
